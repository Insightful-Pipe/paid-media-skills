# How to Use This Skill

Hey Claude, I just added the "ad-demand-test-planner" skill. Plan a paid smoke test for my new offer: tell me which channel, how many visits, what budget, and what result means go.

## What you need

- **Your offer:** what it does, who buys it, the price and the country.
- **The most you'd pay to win one customer,** and roughly how many waitlist sign-ups usually buy. Without them, the skill uses one third of a customer's first-year gross profit and 10%, and says so.
- **A budget cap and a date** you need the answer by.
- **Optional: the InsightfulPipe MCP connected to Claude** with Google Ads, Meta, LinkedIn Ads or GA4. The skill then pulls Keyword Planner bids, Meta audience estimates and your own costs, LinkedIn suggested bids and the test results itself. Without it, paste the same numbers and it does the same math.

## Example requests

**Demand test**
Hey Claude, I just added the "ad-demand-test-planner" skill. I want to know if anyone will pay $49 a month for a meal-planning app for shift workers before I build it. Plan a smoke test with a $1,500 cap.

**Message test**
Hey Claude, I just added the "ad-demand-test-planner" skill. We have three ways to pitch our new add-on. Plan a Facebook ads test that tells us which promise gets the most waitlist sign-ups, and how much it will cost.

**Price test**
Hey Claude, I just added the "ad-demand-test-planner" skill. Test whether $19 or $39 a month gets enough sign-ups to be worth it. Same promise, two prices.

**Read the results**
Hey Claude, I just added the "ad-demand-test-planner" skill. Our smoke test ended yesterday. Pull the spend from Meta and the sign-ups from GA4 and tell me which variants passed the lines we set.

## What you get

1. **The economics:** the most you can pay per sign-up, with the math.
2. **A channel table** for Google Search, Meta, LinkedIn and Reddit: cost per visit, the pass and kill lines, the visits and budget needed, and why each one is chosen or rejected.
3. **The sizing:** visits per variant, total budget, run dates, daily budget per variant, and how big a gap between variants the test can detect.
4. **The variants and setup,** with every ad's tracked link.
5. **The waitlist page brief and the honest note** to people who sign up, word for word.
6. **The rule, frozen before launch.**
7. **After the test, a readout:** each variant marked go, real but short, stop or not read, which promise is proven better, and what the result still doesn't prove.

## What it can change for you

Nothing. It plans and reads the test; you or a campaign builder skill create the campaigns.

## Tips

- Decide the lines before you launch and don't move them. The skill writes them down for you.
- Run one channel per test. If you want Search and Meta, that's two tests with two sets of lines.
- Keep your promise to the people who signed up: tell them the outcome either way.
